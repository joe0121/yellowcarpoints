What it is

Yellow Car Points follows the Corvettes in the IMSA WeatherTech SportsCar Championship: where they stand in the championship, how each race went, and what's happening live during a session, with a focus on the championship fight.

It covers the Corvettes in two classes: GTD PRO (#4, #3, #74) and GTD (#13, #36, #81). Pick a car with the buttons at the top of the Championship or Race page. The site remembers your choice, and you can share a link to a specific car, like yellowcarpoints.win/#13.

It's an unofficial fan project, not affiliated with IMSA, GM, Chevrolet or Corvette Racing.

How to use it

Championship: where we are and what we must do

  • The answer at the top: one line saying what the selected car needs, for example "Win the race and #4 is champion, whatever anyone else does". During the race it becomes where the title stands if the race ended now and the lowest finish the car can afford.
  • Rival by rival: a plain line for each car still in the fight ("if #1 wins, #4 needs P2 or better"), with the full finish-by-finish table one click away. If the car is out of the title fight, the highest championship position it can still reach and what that would take.
  • If the race ended now (race only): the projected championship table, the margin to the title rivals either side, and the title margin lap by lap.
  • Still in contention: every car that can still win the title, with points now and the most it can still reach; in the race, where each is running.
  • Standings, points behind the leader, and race by race (folded away; tap to open) for the background.

Race: what's happening now, for the cars we care about

For the selected car during a session (it shows the last session between events):

  • Session bar: flag, time to go, a one-line title status, and where every Corvette is running (tap one to switch to it).
  • Our cars: the selected car and the cars to watch: position, gap ahead and behind, last lap against its pace, laps to the next stop, and pit-lane status. Add any other car with "Add a car".
  • What just happened: pit stops (time in the lane, fuel, tyres, driver change), position changes and title-rival changes for those cars, newest first. Position changes are the ones seen while the page is open.
  • Timing: lap-time chart for our cars, and the timing table for them and the cars around them (or the full class).
  • Pit strategy and pace: energy, laps left, next stop and fill time per car, and race pace through a stint with a projected matchup.
  • #73 tab: Pratt Miller Motorsports also runs the #73 ORECA in LMP2. Its tab is the same Race page for that car (timing, our cars, pit stops, pace), without championship maths.
  • More detail (folded away): sectors, gap and energy charts, and the strategy board for the whole class.

On race day

During a WeatherTech session the Race tab fills in with our cars, timing, pit strategy and pace, and in the race the Championship tab switches its answer to "if it ended now". Both refresh by themselves (live timing every 10 seconds); no need to reload.

  • In the race, the headline tells you where the title stands if the race ended now, for example "#4 champion by 23 if it ends like this", and the lowest finish the car can afford.
  • The site works out which rivals matter right now. If a different car becomes the closest title rival mid-race, a banner says so.
  • Practice and qualifying show live timing, lap times and pace for the selected car's class. The championship maths only runs in the race.
  • After the checkered flag the result is marked provisional until IMSA publishes the official points, and then the standings update by themselves.

Terms used on the site

If it finished now
Championship points as if the race ended at this moment, using IMSA's points for finishing position (350 for a class win, 320 for 2nd, 300 for 3rd…) plus qualifying points (a tenth of that).
Net order
During a round of pit stops the running order is misleading: a car that has just stopped looks a long way back. The net order moves back the cars that still owe a stop by the time a stop costs, so it shows the order once everyone has pitted. It's switched off under yellow flags.
Cars to watch
On the Race page, the cars compared with the one you picked: the class leader (or the car behind when yours leads), and in the race the closest title rival, just behind (or ahead) in the championship-if-it-finished-now. They update as the race changes, and you can add others.
A place is worth
Points gained by moving up one class position, and lost by dropping one.
Needs P5
The lowest class finish that's still enough, assuming everyone else holds position.
Energy
IMSA meters fuel as "energy" in percent. The site tracks how much each car uses per lap to estimate how far its tank will go.
Stint / pit window
A stint is the run between pit stops. The pit window is the range of laps in which a stop is due, from this race's stints or, early on, last year's race at the same track.
Trend
How lap time or gap is changing per lap over the last 10 laps. Green means quicker or closing, red slower or losing time.

Where the data comes from

  • Championship points and race results: IMSA's official results published by Al Kamel Systems, IMSA's timing provider. Checked every 30 minutes.
  • Live timing: the public feed behind IMSA's live scoring page, read every 10 seconds in the race and every 15 seconds in practice and qualifying.
  • Energy and refuelling: IMSA's public live telemetry, the same data as imsa.com's telemetry page.
  • Session times: IMSA's weekend schedule. The site only follows live timing during WeatherTech sessions.

Limitations

  • Live numbers are estimates. Projections assume everyone holds position and keeps their current pace and energy use. Full-course yellows, short fills, penalties and strategy calls change them. Ties aren't modelled.
  • About 10 to 20 seconds behind, so a little behind TV and IMSA's own timing.
  • Unofficial feeds. If IMSA changes them, parts of the live view can stop working until the site is updated.
  • Hosted on a home server, so the site can occasionally be unavailable.
  • IMSA's results are the final word. If anything here disagrees with them, IMSA is right.
