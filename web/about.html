What it is

Yellow Car Points follows the Corvettes in the IMSA WeatherTech SportsCar Championship: where they stand in the championship, how each race went, and what's happening live during a session, with a focus on the championship fight.

It covers the Pratt Miller-built Corvettes in GTD PRO (#4, #3, #74) and GTD (#13, #36), plus Pratt Miller Motorsports' LMP2 #73. It's a Corvette fan site first; we also follow GM's GTP Cadillacs (#31, #40, #10) and AO Racing (#99 in LMP2, #77 in GTD PRO). Pick This is my car to make any car in any class "our car": the Championship and Race tabs, including the cars to watch, then work for it (without team colours or links). Pick a car with the buttons at the top of the Championship or Race page. Every card has a Hide button to fold it away; the site remembers which cards you've hidden on each device. The page takes on that car's livery colours: Corvette Racing yellow for #3 and #4, black and gold for #13, DXDT blue and green for #36, DragonSpeed's Evel Knievel white, blue and red for #74 and #81, and Pratt Miller's black and orange for the #73 LMP2, and AO Racing's purple "Spike" dragon for the #99 LMP2. The Auto / Light / Dark switch sets the theme. The site remembers your choice, and you can share a link to a specific car, like yellowcarpoints.win/#13.

It's an unofficial fan project, not affiliated with IMSA, GM, Chevrolet or Corvette Racing.

How to use it

Championship: where we are and what we must do

  • The answer at the top: one line saying what the selected car needs, for example "Win the race and #4 is champion, whatever anyone else does". During the race it becomes where the title stands if the race ended now and the lowest finish the car can afford.
  • Rival by rival: a plain line for each car still in the fight ("if #1 wins, #4 needs P2 or better"), with the full finish-by-finish table one click away. If the car is out of the title fight, the highest championship position it can still reach and what that would take.
  • If the race ended now (race only): the projected championship table, the margin to the title rivals either side, and the title margin lap by lap.
  • Championship picture: the top cars' points now, the most each can still reach with the rounds left in its class, and a clinch line: a total no rival can reach any more. In the race, where each is running and its points if it ended now.
  • When the scenarios appear: early in the season the page just shows the picture and the earliest round the leader could clinch. "What #X needs" appears once it matters: when a car can clinch the title at the next round, when the selected car could be knocked out of contention there, and always at the final round.
  • Last race: each Corvette's finish, grid, best lap, stops and points from the latest round; straight after the flag, the provisional result and title outcome from live timing.
  • Standings, points behind the leader, and race by race (folded away; tap to open) for the background.

Race: what's happening now, for the cars we care about

For the selected car during a session (it shows the last session between events):

  • Session bar: flag, time to go, a one-line title status, and where every Corvette is running (tap one to switch to it).
  • Watch: IMSA's own YouTube stream of the session, played by YouTube's player (switch it off with "Turn video off"; the site remembers). In the US the race itself is on Peacock and NBC, and IMSA's YouTube race stream is for viewers outside the US. When IMSA.tv lists an in-car camera for the selected car, a link to it appears here.
  • Our cars: the selected car and the cars to watch: position, gap ahead and behind, last lap against its pace, laps to the next stop, and pit-lane status. Add any other car with "Add a car".
  • What just happened: pit stops (time in the lane, fuel, tyres, driver change), position changes and title-rival changes for those cars, newest first. Everything is recorded by the site's scraper, so reloading the page loses nothing.
  • Track map: where each car is on the track, estimated: GTD PRO and GTD cars from their speed since they crossed the line (IMSA telemetry), LMP2 cars from their last lap time. Our cars are labelled; cars in the pit lane are listed under the map.
  • Weather: the forecast for the race (National Weather Service and Open-Meteo), track and air temperature from IMSA's track weather, a timeline of when conditions change (rain, sunset, dark, cooling) with the estimated lap, flagged when it lines up with your car's next stop, rain and night shading on the lap charts, and pace against track temperature as the race goes on.
  • Race control: IMSA's official race control log: penalties, incidents under review and the decisions, pit lane open/closed and flags, with messages about our cars highlighted (or show only those). It comes from IMSA's results provider after each session and about hourly during long races, so it trails live timing; our cars' penalties and decisions also appear in "What just happened".
  • Drivers: each car's line-up with ratings (Pro = Platinum/Gold, Am = Bronze), drive time so far in the race, who's in the car and for how long, and the Bronze driver's progress towards this event's minimum drive time.
  • Timing: lap-time chart for our cars, and the timing table for them and the cars around them (or the full class).
  • Build a chart: plot gap to the selected car, lap time, lap time against the selected car, gap to the leader, class position, energy or laps into the stint, for our cars, the top 5, the Corvettes or the whole class, with optional smoothing. Yellow flags are shaded and pit stops marked. Add up to four charts; they're remembered on your device.
  • Pit strategy: one row per car: energy left and use per lap, laps left on the tank, next stop, refuel time for the next fill, last stop (F refuel, T tyres, D driver change), stops made and still needed, and pace with its trend through the stint.
  • #73 and #99 (LMP2): Pratt Miller Motorsports' #73 ORECA and AO Racing's #99 "Spike" ORECA, with the LMP2 championship on the Championship tab. LMP2 cars don't send IMSA telemetry, so their energy and track-map position are estimates from timing.
  • More detail (folded away): sectors, gap and energy charts, and the strategy board for the whole class.

Saving your settings and the guest book

Save settings (top of every page) keeps your chosen car, theme, charts and hidden cards, and brings them to your other devices. It's completely anonymous: there's no name, email or password. You get a random sync code to type on another device, and can add a passkey to sign in with your phone's or computer's lock instead. The site stores only your display settings against a scrambled form of the code; you can delete everything from the same panel.

The guest book is open to everyone. Messages appear once they've been approved.

On race day

During a WeatherTech session the Race tab fills in with our cars, timing, pit strategy and pace, and in the race the Championship tab switches its answer to "if it ended now". Both refresh by themselves (live timing every 10 seconds); no need to reload.

  • In the race, the headline tells you where the title stands if the race ended now, for example "#4 champion by 23 if it ends like this", and the lowest finish the car can afford.
  • The site works out which rivals matter right now. If a different car becomes the closest title rival mid-race, a banner says so.
  • Practice and qualifying show live timing, lap times and pace for the selected car's class. The championship maths only runs in the race.
  • After the checkered flag the result is marked provisional until IMSA publishes the official points, and then the standings update by themselves.

Terms used on the site

If it finished now
Championship points as if the race ended at this moment, using IMSA's points for finishing position (350 for a class win, 320 for 2nd, 300 for 3rd…) plus qualifying points (a tenth of that).
Net order
During a round of pit stops the running order is misleading: a car that has just stopped looks a long way back. The net order moves back the cars that still owe a stop by the time a stop costs, so it shows the order once everyone has pitted. It's switched off under yellow flags.
Cars to watch
On the Race page, the cars compared with the one you picked: the class leader (or the car behind when yours leads), and in the race the closest title rival, just behind (or ahead) in the championship-if-it-finished-now. They update as the race changes, and you can add others.
A place is worth
Points gained by moving up one class position, and lost by dropping one.
Needs P5
The lowest class finish that's still enough, assuming everyone else holds position.
Energy
IMSA meters fuel as "energy" in percent. The site tracks how much each car uses per lap to estimate how far its tank will go.
Stint / pit window
A stint is the run between pit stops. The pit window is the range of laps in which a stop is due, from this race's stints or, early on, last year's race at the same track.
Trend
How lap time or gap is changing per lap over the last 10 laps. Green means quicker or closing, red slower or losing time.

Where the data comes from

  • Championship points and race results: IMSA's official results published by Al Kamel Systems, IMSA's timing provider. Checked every 30 minutes.
  • Live timing: the public feed behind IMSA's live scoring page, read every 10 seconds in the race and every 15 seconds in practice and qualifying.
  • Energy and refuelling: IMSA's public live telemetry, the same data as imsa.com's telemetry page.
  • Session times: IMSA's weekend schedule. The site only follows live timing during WeatherTech sessions.

Limitations

  • Live numbers are estimates. Projections assume everyone holds position and keeps their current pace and energy use. Full-course yellows, short fills, penalties and strategy calls change them. Ties aren't modelled.
  • About 10 to 20 seconds behind, so a little behind TV and IMSA's own timing.
  • Unofficial feeds. If IMSA changes them, parts of the live view can stop working until the site is updated.
  • Hosted on a home server, so the site can occasionally be unavailable.
  • IMSA's results are the final word. If anything here disagrees with them, IMSA is right.
